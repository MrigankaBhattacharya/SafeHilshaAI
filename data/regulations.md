# Official Fisheries Regulations & Hilsa Conservation Guidelines
**Jurisdiction:** Department of Fisheries, Government of West Bengal & Cross-Border Riverine Compliance Framework  
**Primary Legal Statues:** West Bengal Inland Fisheries Act, 1984 (Amended 2013, 2015) & Synchronized Conservation Protocol with Bangladesh Protection and Conservation of Fish Act

---

## 1. Hilsa (*Tenualosa ilisha*) Minimum Legal Size Restrictions

### 1.1 The 25-Centimeter Rule
- **Mandatory Catch Size:** Under the West Bengal Inland Fisheries Notification, no individual or commercial fishing vessel is permitted to catch, land, carry, transport, sell, or possess any Hilsa fish measuring less than **25 centimeters (approx. 9.84 inches)** in total length.
- **Definition of 'Jatka' (Juvenile Hilsa):** Any young Hilsa measuring under 25 cm from tip of snout to end of caudal fin is legally classified as **Jatka**.
- **Zero-Tolerance on Jatka:** Harvesting Jatka destroys broodstock recruitment for subsequent years. Possession of Jatka in wholesale markets, landing ghats (e.g., Diamond Harbour, Kakdwip, Godakhali, Namkhana), or retail fish bazaars is strictly illegal and subject to immediate on-the-spot confiscation.

## 2. Mandatory Seasonal Fishing Ban Periods

### 2.1 Peak Spawning Ban (The 22-Day Ashvin-Kartik Window)
- **Timing:** Imposed annually around the full moon of the Bengali month of *Ashvin* to *Kartik* (typically between **October 10 and November 2** depending on lunar alignment).
- **Scope:** Complete blanket ban on catching, transporting, freezing, stocking, and marketing of all Hilsa across all river systems (Bhagirathi-Hooghly, Rupnarayan, Jalangi, Matla, Raimangal) and estuarine coastal zones.
- **Ecological Purpose:** Protects mature female broodstock during peak upstream spawning migration to allow natural fertilization and egg dispersal.

### 2.2 Jatka Conservation Window
- **Timing:** Active from **November 1 to May 31** of every year, with an intensified zero-tolerance enforcement surge during **March and April**.
- **Scope:** Complete restriction on small-mesh fishing gears in primary nursery river stretches.

### 2.3 Annual Marine Fishing Ban (Bay of Bengal Coast)
- **Timing:** **April 15 to June 14 (61 consecutive days)** every year.
- **Scope:** All motorized fishing trawlers and mechanized boats are prohibited from operating in coastal territorial waters and deep-sea exclusive economic zones (EEZ) to allow multi-species regeneration.

## 3. Designated River Sanctuaries & Restricted Geo-Zones

The Department of Fisheries has notified specific critical river stretches as **Permanent or Seasonal Hilsa Sanctuaries**. Fishing within these demarcated boundaries is strictly prohibited:

### Sanctuary Zone A: Farakka Barrage Protected Zone
- **Location & Extent:** 5 kilometers upstream and 5 kilometers downstream of the Farakka Barrage on the Ganges/Bhagirathi River (Districts: Murshidabad and Malda).
- **Coordinates:** Centered around 24.80°N, 87.92°E.
- **Status:** **RESTRICTED (Year-Round Permanent Buffer)**.
- **Reason:** The Farakka fish-lock and upstream/downstream channels are the primary bottlenecks where migrating Hilsa congregate; harvesting here decimates migrating broodstock.

### Sanctuary Zone B: Katwa to Lalbagh River Sanctuary
- **Location & Extent:** Bhagirathi River stretch from Katwa (Purba Bardhaman) upstream through Nabadwip to Lalbagh (Murshidabad).
- **Coordinates:** Covering 23.40°N to 23.65°N, 88.20°E to 88.35°E.
- **Status:** **RESTRICTED (Intensive Spawning Reserve)**.
- **Reason:** Shallow gravel and sandbar riverbeds serve as prime natural breeding grounds and larval dispersion nurseries.

### Sanctuary Zone C: Diamond Harbour to Sagar Island Estuarine Corridor
- **Location & Extent:** Lower Hooghly estuarine funnel from Diamond Harbour south to Sagar Island, Kulpi, and Fraserganj.
- **Coordinates:** Covering 21.65°N to 22.20°N, 88.05°E to 88.25°E.
- **Status:** **RESTRICTED during designated migration windows & Jatka transit**.
- **Reason:** Critical estuarine transition corridor where juvenile Hilsa adjust osmolarity before migrating to marine waters.

### Sanctuary Zone D: Sundarbans Matla-Raimangal Delta Conservation Zone
- **Location & Extent:** Estuarine waterways of the Sundarbans Biosphere Reserve encompassing Matla, Bidya, and Raimangal rivers.
- **Coordinates:** Covering 21.80°N to 22.15°N, 88.65°E to 89.05°E.
- **Status:** **RESTRICTED (Marine protected delta area)**.
- **Reason:** Dense mangrove channels serving as vital shelter and nursery ecosystems for juvenile fingerlings.

## 4. Prohibited Fishing Gear & Net Mesh Size Specifications

### 4.1 Banned Gear
- **Monofilament Nylon Gill Nets ("Current Jal"):** Fully outlawed under all circumstances. Any net made from single-strand synthetic nylon fiber of mesh size below **90 mm (3.54 inches)** is prohibited.
- **Zero-Mesh Bag Nets (Behundi Jal / Charpata Jal):** Strictly banned in estuarine choke points because they trap fry and juvenile fish of all species indiscriminately.
- **Trawling within 8 km of Shoreline:** Mechanized bottom trawling is banned in nearshore coastal waters.

### 4.2 Permitted Eco-Friendly Gear
- Traditional drift gill nets (*Chandi Jal*) with diamond mesh size greater than **90 mm (3.5 inches)**.
- Hook-and-line (*Barsi*) for non-restricted species.
- Traditional cast nets (*Khepla Jal*) operating in open community waters for indigenous carps.

## 5. Legal Enforcement & Penalties for Violations

Violations of the West Bengal Inland Fisheries Act and corresponding marine notifications entail immediate executive penalties:

1. **First Offense:**
   - On-the-spot confiscation and public destruction of illegal monofilament nets ("Current Jal").
   - Confiscation of entire catch without compensation; legal fish auctioned or donated to welfare facilities.
   - Compounding monetary fine of **₹5,000 to ₹10,000**.
   - Temporary suspension of vessel operating license (up to 30 days).

2. **Repeat Offense / Willful Breach of Sanctuary:**
   - Permanent impoundment and seizure of fishing trawler/boat and outboard engines.
   - Criminal prosecution before the Sub-Divisional Judicial Magistrate (SDJM).
   - Punishable with **rigorous imprisonment up to 1 to 2 years** and fines up to **₹25,000**.
   - Forfeiture of all state subsidies, fuel permits, and fisheries cooperative memberships.

## 6. Sustainable Alternative Fish Species (Legal Harvesting)

During official Hilsa ban periods or when fishing near protected river corridors, fishermen are strongly encouraged to redirect fishing efforts towards abundant, commercially viable, and non-protected fresh and brackish water species:

| Common Name | Bengali Name | Scientific Name | Legal Status | Recommended Fishing Gear |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rohu / Rui** | রুই | *Labeo rohita* | ✅ LEGAL & Abundant | Traditional seine / large mesh gill net |
| **Catla / Katla** | কাতলা | *Gibelion catla* | ✅ LEGAL & High Value | Surface gill net (mesh > 100mm) |
| **Mrigal** | মৃগেল | *Cirrhinus mrigala* | ✅ LEGAL & Abundant | Bottom drift net / hook-and-line |
| **Tilapia / Nile Tilapia** | তেলাপিয়া | *Oreochromis niloticus* | ✅ UNRESTRICTED | Cast net / gill net (no season limit) |
| **Pangas Catfish** | পাঙ্গাস | *Pangasius pangasius* | ✅ LEGAL in river channels | Longline / bottom gill net |
| **Bata Carp** | বাটা | *Labeo bata* | ✅ LEGAL | Mesh size > 50 mm |
| **Tangra / Freshwater Catfish**| টেংরা | *Batasio tengana* / *Mystus sp.* | ✅ LEGAL | Small baited traps / hand cast net |

### Sustainable Fishermen Guidance & Community Livelihoods:
- **Livelihood Compensation (VGF Scheme):** Registered traditional fishermen observing the Hilsa spawning ban are entitled to government food security assistance (40 kg rice per family per month during the ban).
- **Aquaculture Diversification:** Local cooperatives provide seed and feed subsidies for composite carp culture (Rui, Catla, Mrigal) and cage aquaculture in inland oxbow lakes (*beels* and *baors*).
- **Emergency Reporting:** Report illegal Jatka netting or sanctuary poaching to the State Fisheries Control Room (Toll-Free: 1800-345-3224) or local Coastal Police stations.
